### Report

Rishabh Pant and Dhruv Jurel crafted resilient half-centuries under pressure, forging an unbroken 112-run partnership for the seventh wicket to propel India to 419-6 at lunch on Day 2 of the second Test against Sri Lanka at Colombo’s Sinhalese Sports Club on Monday. Resuming at 300-5, India lost debutant Saransh Jain early for six, but Pant (63 not out) and Jurel (59 not out) absorbed hostility and capitalized on their chances to shift momentum decisively in the tourists’ favor.

The session began with Sri Lanka striking immediately via Asitha Fernando, who dismissed Jain with a sharp bouncer that induced a top-edge to gully. At 307-6, concerns lingered over India’s lower order, but Pant—returning after retiring hurt on Day 1 due to a blow to his forearm—walked in to face a fired-up Sri Lankan pace attack. Lahiru Kumara and Fernando targeted Pant with short-pitched deliveries, forcing him to take blows to the shoulder, but the left-hander combatted aggression with composure. He pulled Kumara for a six that cleared the stadium roof, necessitating a replacement ball, and later accelerated to his 21st Test fifty off 81 deliveries, including six boundaries and two sixes. Jurel, meanwhile, anchored his innings with disciplined defense and crisp drives, reaching his third Test half-century with a pull through mid-wicket.

Sri Lanka’s bowlers, despite persistent efforts, struggled to contain the pair as Pant and Jurel rotated the strike efficiently and punished errant deliveries. Fernando, the pick of the bowlers with 4-60, and Kumara (1-61) faced resistance even after Sri Lanka turned to spinners Prabath Jayasuriya and Keshara Nuwantha. The duo’s partnership not only stabilized India’s innings but also eclipsed the psychological boost Sri Lanka had hoped to carry from the morning.

The foundation for India’s recovery was laid on Day 1 by Devdutt Padikkal, whose 117—his second consecutive Test century—helped the visitors reach 300-5 after captain Shubman Gill elected to bat. Padikkal, who scored 167 in the first Test, spent nearly five hours at the crease, surviving two early drops, and stitched crucial stands of 120 runs with Gill (50) and 74 runs with Ravindra Jadeja (43). Sri Lanka’s bowlers, led by Fernando’s 3-46, maintained discipline on a pitch offering early movement but faded as the day progressed.

Sri Lanka’s bowling coach, Ryan van Niekerk, acknowledged the challenge post-lunch on Day 1, saying, “I thought we fought really well today, fought hard,” while expressing hope that the new ball on Day 2 could spark a breakthrough. However, Pant and Jurel neutralized that threat, leaving Sri Lanka to regroup ahead of the afternoon session.

With India already holding a 1-0 lead in the two-match series after their 165-run victory in Galle, the seventh-wicket duo’s resolve has positioned the tourists to dictate terms in the series finale. Yet, the match remains poised, as Sri Lanka will aim to restrict further damage and counter strongly in their reply.
