### Report

Rescue workers in Zamboye village, Central African Republic, retrieved another body from the rubble of an artisanal gold mine early Wednesday, bringing the confirmed death toll to over 100, according to local officials and a Red Cross volunteer involved in the efforts. The mine, located in the Nana-Mambere prefecture near the Cameroon border, collapsed Tuesday morning, trapping dozens beneath tons of earth and debris. (ap_india)  

The disaster, described by Bertrand Be Yangué, a member of the Zamboye youth council, as a scene of “heartbreak and despair,” has left survivors and relatives grappling with the scale of loss. “Finding them alive would be a miracle,” Yangué said, referring to the several individuals still unaccounted for. Gervais Ganagoroh, a Red Cross volunteer, confirmed that multiple survivors were critically injured, with limited medical resources available in the remote area. (ap_india)  

The mine, like many in the region, operated without formal safety regulations, relying on manual labor and rudimentary tools to extract gold. Footage from the site showed miners, many of them young men and women coated in mud, digging through debris while others shouted for help amid the sprawling excavation area. Artisanal mining is a common livelihood in the Central African Republic, where such collapses are frequent due to inadequate protective measures. (ap_india)  

Prosecutors in the Baboua sub-prefecture have launched an investigation to determine the cause of the collapse, identify victims, and assess potential criminal liability for those overseeing the site. Authorities have not yet disclosed whether the mine was operating legally or if it had been inspected for safety compliance. (ap_india)  

The death toll, already exceeding 100, may still rise as search operations continue, according to both the Red Cross and local officials. Reuters corroborated the casualty figures, noting that communication challenges and the site’s inaccessibility have slowed efforts to confirm the full extent of the tragedy. (reuters_india)  

This incident underscores the perilous conditions faced by artisanal miners in the Central African Republic, where gold extraction often occurs in unstable, unregulated environments. While such mines provide crucial income for communities, they frequently lack the infrastructure to prevent accidents, leading to recurring fatalities.

As of Wednesday, the focus remains on recovering bodies and providing aid to survivors, though heavy rains threatened to further destabilize the site. The investigation’s outcome and the final death toll remain pending.
