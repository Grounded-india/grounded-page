### Report

Union Minister of Coal and Mines G. Kishan Reddy on Sunday launched the first sector-wide corporate social responsibility framework for Indian coal companies, an effort to standardise and coordinate community investment across the industry (pib). The event, held in New Delhi, also marked milestones under two health-focused programmes — the Thalassemia Bal Sewa Yojana and the Nanha Sa Dil initiative — linking mining-sector CSR spending to measurable public-health outcomes.

The new framework is intended to replace the fragmented approach in which individual coal public-sector undertakings designed and executed CSR projects independently. By establishing common guidelines, reporting templates and priority areas, the ministry aims to ensure that funds mandated under the Companies Act are directed toward high-impact interventions in coal-bearing regions, many of which overlap with aspirational districts identified by NITI Aayog. Officials said the framework will also introduce a unified monitoring dashboard so that the ministry, state governments and local communities can track project progress in real time.

During the ceremony, Reddy highlighted the progress of the Thalassemia Bal Sewa Yojana, which provides financial assistance for bone-marrow transplants and lifelong care for children suffering from thalassemia major. The programme, supported by coal PSUs, has so far facilitated transplants for over 300 children across multiple states, according to ministry data shared at the event. The minister also pointed to the Nanha Sa Dil initiative, which funds congenital heart surgery for children from economically weaker families in mining areas. More than 1,200 surgeries have been performed since the programme’s inception, with survival rates comparable to national benchmarks.

Both health schemes were cited as templates for the kind of targeted, outcome-oriented projects the new CSR framework seeks to replicate. Reddy told attendees that the coal sector’s annual CSR outlay — estimated at over ₹1,500 crore — should move beyond infrastructure handouts such as school buildings and water tanks toward interventions with verifiable health, education and livelihood metrics. He urged coal companies to adopt the framework immediately and align their annual action plans with its guidelines before the next financial year.

The launch comes as the ministry faces pressure to demonstrate that coal-producing districts, which bear the environmental and social costs of mining, receive a proportionate share of development benefits. While the framework sets out principles and reporting requirements, it does not prescribe a fixed allocation formula for individual companies, leaving PSUs to propose projects within the defined priority sectors. Ministry officials said compliance will be reviewed quarterly and that non-adherence could affect performance evaluations of PSU boards.

Stakeholders in coal-bearing states will be watching whether the unified dashboard translates into faster fund disbursement and fewer duplicated projects. The first quarterly review under the new framework is scheduled for December.
