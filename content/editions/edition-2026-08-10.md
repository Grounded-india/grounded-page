### Report

Thousands of protesters converged on the Jharkhand state assembly in Ranchi yesterday, demanding reforms in the government recruitment process amid allegations of irregularities in a civil service exam conducted by the Jharkhand Public Service Commission (JPSC) in April (the_hindu). The demonstration, which has drawn participation from students, job seekers, and political groups, marks the latest escalation in a wave of youth-led agitation over perceived corruption in public examinations across India.

The immediate trigger for the protest is the controversy surrounding the JPSC’s preliminary civil service exam results, released in July. Candidates alleged that leaked answer sheets circulating on social media revealed successful applicants had answered disproportionately few questions, sparking suspicions of manipulation. The state government has initiated an investigation by the Criminal Investigation Department (CID), leading to over a dozen arrests, including individuals linked to a private company involved in the exam process (google_news_india). Following talks with protesters on Sunday, the government agreed to cancel the disputed exam along with two others, and three JPSC members, including former chairman L Khiangte, have resigned (the_hindu).

Despite these concessions, demonstrators insist their agitation will continue until all demands are met, particularly the transfer of the investigation to the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI). The protest has also drawn political backing: the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), which opposes the ruling coalition in Jharkhand, has staged solidarity demonstrations and raised the issue in the state assembly, while the Congress party, a national rival of the BJP but part of the state government, has cautiously expressed support for the students’ grievances (google_news_india).

The Ranchi protests echo broader frustrations among India’s youth, who have increasingly mobilized over exam irregularities and unemployment. Last month, the so-called “Cockroach Janta Party” (CJP) movement, a decentralized student-led campaign, called off its five-week-long nationwide demonstrations after the resignation of federal education minister Dharmendra Pradhan (google_news_india). Unlike the CJP’s apolitical stance, however, the Jharkhand agitation has seen active participation from established political parties, reflecting its localized intensity and the state’s charged electoral landscape.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi, meanwhile, addressed the broader context of youth unrest indirectly yesterday. In a Sanskrit Subhashitam shared on social media, he praised the “effort and dedication of India’s youth,” while separately urging participation in the Har Ghar Tiranga movement, a campaign to promote the national flag ahead of Independence Day (pib-response). The remarks, though not explicitly referencing the protests, underscore the government’s effort to frame youth energy within a nationalist narrative amid growing discontent over employment and governance issues.

As the standoff in Ranchi persists, protesters remain camped at a local stadium, with some on hunger strike and others sleeping in the open. The state government’s ability to address the core demand for a federal investigation remains uncertain, leaving the path to resolution fraught with both legal and political complexities.

The incident underscores ongoing efforts by Indian regulatory bodies to enforce stringent safety protocols across transportation sectors. Passengers and cabin crew on board AI 2379 reported sudden turbulence during the flight, though the exact sequence of events remains under review. The airline has not publicly commented on the incident beyond acknowledging the DGCA’s actions. As of Monday, the confirmatory drug test results for the captain remain pending, and the AAIB’s investigation is in its preliminary stages.
