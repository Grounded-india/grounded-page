### Report

Foreign investors have ramped up short positions on Indian equities, prompting brokerages to warn of a weak start to October after the benchmark indices logged their worst month since March. The selling pressure comes as elevated oil prices and a global repricing of interest-rate expectations triggered sustained outflows through September, leaving the rupee near the bottom of the Asian currency pack and bond yields hovering around multi-decade highs (reuters_india).

The Nifty 50 and Sensex ended the month slightly lower on Friday, capping a losing streak driven by the twin drag of surging crude and a resilient dollar. While equities showed relative resilience compared with the bruising selloff in domestic bonds, the buildup of short positions by foreign portfolio investors suggests the defensive posture may extend into the new month. Analysts cited by Reuters said the increased shorts reflect a tactical hedge against further downside rather than a structural exit, though the depth of the positioning could amplify any negative catalyst (reuters_india).

The market turbulence unfolded against a busy diplomatic backdrop. Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal arrived in Milwaukee on Tuesday for the G20 Trade Ministers' Meeting, where discussions are expected to focus on supply-chain resilience and digital trade rules (pib-response). Separately, the Prime Minister held talks with the President of Iran on the sidelines of the BRICS Summit, underscoring New Delhi's engagement with the expanded grouping amid shifting geopolitical alignments (pib-response).

Domestically, policy attention remained fixed on digital infrastructure. At the Aadhaar Samvaad in Kolkata, the Unique Identification Authority of India unveiled five new initiatives aimed at strengthening trusted digital identity and governance frameworks, including enhanced authentication layers and consent-based data-sharing protocols (pib-response). The moves are part of a broader push to deepen the digital public infrastructure stack that underpins welfare delivery and financial inclusion.

Adding to the macroeconomic uncertainty, the southwest monsoon concluded the 2026 season with 87 per cent of the long-period average, marking the weakest rainfall since 2015. The India Meteorological Department reported that 313 of 741 districts recorded deficient or largely deficient rainfall, with 71 districts receiving less than half their normal quota. The shortfall, concentrated in the east, northeast and southern peninsula, has raised concerns over kharif output and potential food-price pressures heading into the festival quarter (google_news_india).

The Reserve Bank of India's foreign-exchange forward book, which touched $200 billion earlier in the year, is now tipped to shrink as the central bank manages rupee volatility amid the capital outflows (google_news_india). With the monsoon deficit firming up inflation risks and global rates staying higher for longer, the policy window for rate cuts appears to be narrowing. Market participants will watch the RBI's October policy review for signals on whether the central bank prioritises growth support or currency stability in the coming months.

The meeting signals a unified strategy to challenge the EC's decisions through both parliamentary confrontation and potential legal action, marking a significant escalation in the ongoing dispute over electoral integrity.

### The debate

**The Opposition (INDIA bloc):** The INDIA bloc's convergence in Delhi yesterday marks the first time the full spectrum of Opposition parties — from the Trinamool and Congress to the Left and regional allies — have treated the Election Commission not as a neutral referee but as an active participant in electoral manipulation. Mamata Banerjee's direct charge that Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar and his "full software team" cheated the electorate, coupled with the bloc's demand for his immediate removal and a prepared parliamentary motion, signals a strategic shift: the Opposition is no longer litigating individual booth-level irregularities but targeting the institutional architecture that enabled them. The Special Intensive Revision exercise in Madhya Pradesh, Haryana, Bihar, and West Bengal is cited as the mechanism where mass deletions and bogus insertions were engineered under the CEC's watch. (the_hindu, google_news_india)

**CEC Gyanesh Kumar / Election Commission:** The Opposition's opening conflates political disagreement with institutional failure, citing no specific statutory violation by the Commission — only the Special Intensive Revision exercise they themselves demanded in multiple states to clean bloated rolls. The Election Commission conducts revisions under the Representation of the People Act, 1950, with draft rolls published for public scrutiny, claims and objections heard by Electoral Registration Officers, and final orders appealable to the District Election Officer and Chief Electoral Officer — a quasi-judicial process the Opposition now bypasses by demanding the CEC's removal via parliamentary motion. No court has stayed the SIR exercise; no Election Petition has established "mass deletions" as systemic fraud rather than statutory housekeeping. The Commission's mandate is to prepare accurate rolls, not to satisfy political consensus on outcomes.

**The Opposition (INDIA bloc) (rebuttal):** The Commission's defence rests on procedural correctness — the Representation of the People Act, draft rolls, claims and objections — but that is precisely the framework the Opposition alleges was weaponised. Mamata Banerjee's charge yesterday is not that the SIR lacked legal cover; it is that the "full software team" under Gyanesh Kumar engineered mass deletions and bogus insertions in Madhya Pradesh, Haryana, Bihar and West Bengal *within* that cover, turning statutory housekeeping into targeted disenfranchisement. (the_hindu, google_news_india)

Rahul Gandhi and Tejashwi Yadav had already flagged the Bihar pattern before this meeting; the INDIA bloc's convergence yesterday — Trinamool, Congress, Left, regional allies — produced a draft parliamentary notice for the CEC's removal because the quasi-judicial remedies the Commission cites are exhausted when the umpire himself is accused of designing the deletion logic. (google_news_india)

The Opposition's demand is not for the EC to "satisfy political consensus on outcomes" but for the CEC to answer specific allegations of vote theft in four states before the same Parliament that can remove him under Article 324(5).

**CEC Gyanesh Kumar / Election Commission (closing):** The Opposition's rebuttal confirms the core problem: they have moved from alleging specific, verifiable irregularities in the Special Intensive Revision to imputing motive to the CEC's "full software team" without a single forensic audit, Election Petition, or court order to substantiate "engineered mass deletions." Rahul Gandhi and Tejashwi Yadav flagged Bihar; Mamata Banerjee now adds three more states — but the evidentiary standard for removing a Constitutional authority under Article 324(5) is "proved misbehaviour or incapacity," not a draft parliamentary notice prepared ahead of proof. The Commission's quasi-judicial machinery — EROs, DEOs, CEOs, and ultimately the High Courts under Article 226 — exists precisely to test these claims with evidence; bypassing it for a political motion suggests the Opposition knows the legal record will not sustain the rhetoric.

**Bottom line:** Opposition leaders converged in Delhi yesterday to demand CEC Gyanesh Kumar's removal, alleging his "full software team" engineered mass deletions and bogus insertions during the Special Intensive Revision in Madhya Pradesh, Haryana, Bihar, and West Bengal (the_hindu, google_news_india). The Election Commission counters that the SIR was conducted under the Representation of the People Act with statutory quasi-judicial safeguards — draft rolls, claims and objections, and appeals — and that no court has stayed the exercise or found systemic fraud. ## 3. India Secures Gold Medals in Relay and Compound Archery at Asian Games

*Yesterday, Indian athletes claimed multiple gold medals at the 2026 Asian Games, highlighted by a victory in the women's 4x400m relay and dominant performances in compound archery.*

![India's women's compound archery team proudly displays their gold medals at the 2026 Asian Games.](images/2026-09-30/8c6f690d-023c-4bbb-96da-04447735da15-0-e6e8a6419e3c.jpg)

<sub>*India's women's compound archery team proudly displays their gold medals at the 2026 Asian Games. — Photo via [The Hindu](https://sportstar.thehindu.com/asian-games/india-medal-tally-live-asian-games-2026-day-12-september-30-gold-silver-bronze/article71526545.ece)*</sub>

> REPORT  ·  16 sources  ·  9 claim(s) kept  ·  4 verified

### Context

India continued its strong showing at the 2026 Asian Games on September 30, adding multiple gold medals across shooting and archery. Kynan Chenai and Neeru Dhanda secured the top spot in the trap mixed team shooting event, while the compound archery contingent delivered three golds, including a world record-equalling performance by the women's team and a mixed team victory for Chikitha Taniparthi and Sahil Jadhav. On the track, the women's 4x400m relay squad of Kiran Pahal, Poovamma MR, Prachi, and Vithya Ramraj also competed as India maintained its position atop the medal tally.

### Report

On a rain-slicked Tuesday night at Nagoya’s Mizuho Stadium, India’s women’s 4x400m relay team lined up for a race that had been framed around a single opponent. Bahrain’s Salwa Eid Naser, the Olympic silver medallist and individual 400m champion at these Games, was the anchor the field had been dreading. She took the baton for the final lap with a lead, her stride projecting the inevitability that had defined her season. Vithya Ramraj, India’s anchor, had spent the preceding laps watching her teammates — Kiran Pahal, Poovamma MR, and Prachi — build a margin designed to survive Naser’s closing speed. The plan, as Pahal later recalled, was explicit: give Vithya 50 metres (the_hindu). It held. India crossed in 3:28.45, securing a seventh relay gold in eight Asian Games editions and denying Naser the anchor-leg spectacle the stadium had anticipated (the_hindu).

The victory carried weight beyond the track. For Pahal, 26, the gold arrived four years after her father, Om Prakash, a farmer from Ghummad village in Haryana’s Sonepat district, passed away. He had once gifted her a gold necklace, telling her it was a placeholder until she won a medal of her own. She wore that chain beneath her India vest in Nagoya, tucking it away before the final (the_hindu). Prime Minister Narendra Modi congratulated the quartet by name on Wednesday, marking the relay as a centrepiece of the day’s haul (pib-response).

By Wednesday morning, the focus had shifted to the archery range at Okazaki Chuo Sogo Park, where India completed a clean sweep of the three compound team titles. The women’s trio of Jyothi Surekha Vennam, Chikitha Taniparthi, and Prithika Pradeep opened the account by equalling the world record of 238 in the final against China, defending the crown they won in Hangzhou (the_hindu). The score matched the mark set by South Korea at the 2014 Incheon Games (google_news_india). The final was preceded by a half-hour rain delay caused by the late arrival of the Chinese team bus; India, asked to shoot first against Korea in the semifinal, accepted without disruption. Coach Jiwanjot Singh later noted the squad had replicated the Okazaki range — down to the flag positions and background music — at a training camp in Sonepat, including wet-weather sessions that neutralised Tuesday’s downpour (the_hindu).

Chikitha returned hours later to pair with Sahil Jadhav in the mixed team final against Korea. The match swung on a final-end sequence where a 9 from Chikitha left India needing two 10s; Sahil delivered his, Chikitha followed with a 10, and the shoot-off finished 158-157 after both Indians hit the centre ring while Korea’s Choi Eun Gyu managed a 9 (google_news_india). The win carried a 2028 Los Angeles Olympic quota place, the first for India in compound mixed team (google_news_india).

The men’s compound team — Kushal Dalal, Sahil Jadhav, and Ganesh Mani Ratnam Thirumuru — closed the hat-trick by overturning a two-point deficit against China. Trailing 116-118 after three ends, India posted three 10s in the fourth while China’s Jiahao Liao dropped an 8, sealing a 238-237 win with three Chinese arrows unshot (google_news_india). It was India’s third consecutive Asian Games gold in the event.

Earlier Wednesday, the shooting range delivered the day’s first gold. Kynan Chenai and Neeru Dhanda broke the Asian Games record in trap mixed team with a score of 34, surpassing the previous Games mark of 30. Qatar took silver with 32, China bronze with 23 (google_news_india). It was India’s first-ever gold in the discipline at the Asiad and the ninth of the campaign for the shooting contingent, which now stands at 15 medals overall (google_news_india). Neeru had already made history Tuesday as the first Indian woman to win an individual trap gold at the Games, adding a team silver alongside Aashima Ahlawat and Manisha Keer (google_news_india).

The medal ledger reflected the breadth of the day. By evening, India’s tally stood at 56 medals — 9 gold, 21 silver, 26 bronze — according to the official tracker (google_news_india). Other sources placed the count at 60 with 10 gold after the men’s compound victory (the_hindu). The discrepancy underscores the fluidity of a programme still unfolding across athletics, boxing, shooting, archery, squash, and hockey through October 4. In boxing, Priya Ghanghas settled for bronze in the women’s 60kg after a 1-4 semifinal loss to China’s Yang Chengyu, while Kapil Pokhariya took bronze in the men’s 90kg (the_hindu). In wrestling, Sunil Kumar opened India’s account with a bronze-medal bout win (the_hindu).
