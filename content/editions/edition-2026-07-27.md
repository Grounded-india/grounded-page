## 1. Government Appoints Nandan Nilekani to Head Exam Reform Task Force

*Contested: 5 outlet(s), no primary source. Prime Minister Narendra Modi announced the high-powered panel to make India's examination system leak-proof and technology-driven following the introduction of a new anti-paper leak bill.*

> DEBATE  ·  24 sources  ·  8 claim(s) kept

### Context

The government appointed Infosys co-founder Nandan Nilekani to lead a task force on exam reform and introduced the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill in the Lok Sabha. These steps follow a series of high-profile paper leaks and irregularities in national entrance and recruitment tests that eroded public confidence in the examination system. The legislative and institutional response aims to strengthen safeguards against cheating while the Nilekani panel is expected to recommend structural and technological changes to restore credibility.

### The debate

**The Government:** Appointing Nandan Nilekani to head the exam reform task force signals a decisive shift toward structural solutions rather than political patchwork. As the architect of Aadhaar, he brings proven experience in building large-scale, tamper-resistant digital infrastructure — precisely the capability the examination system lacks. The Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill now before Lok Sabha supplies the necessary legal teeth, criminalising organised leaking and enabling technology-driven prevention. Together, the legislative framework and the technical leadership address the supply side of the crisis. The remaining challenge is execution at speed, and the government has put the right combination of authority and expertise in place to deliver it.

**The Opposition:** Appointing a technologist to chair a committee does not substitute for the political accountability that has been absent through multiple paper leaks across SSC, NEET, and railway recruitment exams. The Public Examinations Amendment Bill criminalises leaks after they occur but does not address the systemic opacity in question-paper setting, printing, and transportation where the breaches originate. Nilekani’s Aadhaar experience involved enrolling voluntary users into a biometric database; it did not involve securing a high-stakes, adversarial examination supply chain that spans thousands of centres and multiple state agencies. The government’s own track record — cancelling NEET-UG 2024 only after Supreme Court intervention and delaying the CBI probe into the NEET paper leak — shows that the bottleneck is political will, not technical blueprints. A committee report without a fixed timeline for implementation and an independent audit of the National Testing Agency is another deferral of responsibility.

**The Government (rebuttal):** The Opposition claims the Bill only criminalises leaks after they occur, but the Amendment explicitly empowers the Centre to notify technology-driven prevention standards for paper-setting, printing, and transportation — the very supply-chain stages they identify as vulnerable (indian_express). They argue Nilekani’s Aadhaar experience is irrelevant because it enrolled voluntary users, yet Aadhaar’s core achievement was building a deduplicated, tamper-resistant identity stack at 1.3 billion scale under adversarial fraud attempts — precisely the architectural challenge of securing high-stakes exam logistics across thousands of centres (the_hindu). The NEET-UG 2024 cancellation followed a Supreme Court directive, but the government had already referred the leak to the CBI and removed the NTA Director General before the Court’s final order, demonstrating administrative action preceding judicial compulsion (indian_express). The task force has been mandated to submit its report within a fixed timeline, and the Amendment provides for independent audit mechanisms the Opposition asserts are missing (the_hindu).

**The Opposition (closing):** The government claims the Amendment empowers technology-driven prevention standards, but the Bill's text still relies on post-facto criminalisation rather than mandating end-to-end encryption or blockchain custody logs for the paper supply chain — a gap the sources do not refute. They equate Aadhaar's deduplication architecture with exam logistics, yet Aadhaar's threat model was identity fraud by enrollees, not insider collusion across printing presses, transport contractors, and centre superintendents where the NEET and SSC leaks actually occurred. The CBI referral and NTA Director General's removal came weeks after the leak surfaced and only after public outcry forced the government's hand, not as proactive administrative action. A task force report deadline without a public implementation tracker and an audit mechanism still controlled by the Ministry that oversees the NTA is structural insulation, not accountability.

**Bottom line:** Both sides agree that Nandan Nilekani has been appointed to head an exam reform task force and that the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill has been introduced in Lok Sabha (indian_express, the_hindu). They disagree on whether the Bill’s provision for technology-driven prevention standards meaningfully secures the paper supply chain or merely adds post-facto criminal penalties, and on whether Nilekani’s Aadhaar experience translates to securing an adversarial exam logistics network. The government asserts the task force has a fixed deadline and the Amendment enables independent audits (the_hindu), while the Opposition contends the audit mechanism remains under the Ministry overseeing the NTA and lacks a public implementation tracker. The load-bearing question is whether structural reforms can restore credibility without political accountability for past leaks.

## 2. Indian Banks Raise $32 Billion Through Dollar-Inflow Schemes

*The Reserve Bank of India reported that recent measures to attract foreign capital have successfully mobilized $32 billion, largely through FCNR(B) deposits.*

> REPORT  ·  3 sources  ·  3 claim(s) kept  ·  2 verified

### Context

Indian banks have raised $32 billion through special dollar-inflow schemes, Reserve Bank of India Governor Sanjay Malhotra disclosed in an interview with Hindu Businessline. The fundraising reflects ongoing efforts to bolster foreign currency reserves and support the banking sector's liquidity position.

### Report

Reserve Bank of India Governor Sanjay Malhotra said Indian banks have mobilised nearly $32 billion through special dollar-inflow schemes, with the bulk raised via Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) or FCNR(B) deposits (the_hindu). In an interview with The Hindu BusinessLine, Malhotra said the inflows reflect a strong investor response to recent central bank measures designed to attract foreign capital and bolster the country’s external buffers (the_hindu).

The fundraising comes alongside more than $7 billion in foreign inflows into government securities since the RBI’s June policy announcements (the_hindu). Malhotra dismissed suggestions that the fresh deposits merely represent a recycling of existing funds, arguing that the response indicates genuine new capital entering the system (the_hindu). He added that the central bank possesses adequate tools to manage any liquidity consequences arising from the surge in foreign currency holdings (the_hindu).

Addressing concerns that the RBI bears the hedging cost on new FCNR(B) deposits and offers concessional foreign exchange swaps for external commercial borrowings by public sector entities, Malhotra said the risk is contained. “It is not something which should be a matter of concern because we have a foolproof system of insuring ourselves. So, whatever dollars we get, the excess foreign currency is invested in foreign assets. The risk, therefore, is not there,” he said (the_hindu).

The Governor framed the measures as a response to challenging global conditions facing emerging markets, citing heightened geopolitical uncertainty and volatile capital flows (the_hindu). He said the inflows have strengthened India’s external position and are expected to further support the balance of payments and currency stability (the_hindu).

On the rupee’s recent depreciation, Malhotra argued the move does not reflect weakness in domestic economic fundamentals. He attributed pressure on the currency to geopolitical tensions, broad dollar strength and volatility across emerging markets (the_hindu). “We do not target any specific exchange rate or band for the rupee. Our intervention, whenever necessary, is targeted to curb excessive volatility,” he said, adding that the currency is “not overvalued” and could be considered undervalued in both nominal and real effective exchange rate terms (the_hindu).

Malhotra pointed to a current account surplus during April and May, robust services exports, resilient remittance inflows, rising merchandise exports and improving foreign direct investment as indicators of external sector strength (the_hindu). On reserve management, he said the RBI continues to be guided by the principles of safety, liquidity and returns, with periodic reviews of deployment (the_hindu).

Turning to monetary policy, the Governor reiterated that inflation control remains the foremost priority even as the central bank remains mindful of growth risks. He said the Monetary Policy Committee will maintain a data-dependent approach while navigating the evolving growth-inflation trade-off. “Our primary mandate is inflation and price stability. Therefore, we will do whatever is required first, to keep price stability and then, to see to what extent we can support growth,” he said (the_hindu).

**Sources:** Google News India, Reuters India, The Hindu

Iran subsequently halted its own counterattacks, which had targeted bases hosting U.S. forces in Jordan and Iraq, killing at least four service members in recent weeks (ap_india). Despite the lull in kinetic action, Iranian officials struck a skeptical tone regarding formal negotiations. Tehran also asserted it retains control over the Strait of Hormuz, the flashpoint for the recent escalation (reuters_india).

Regional mediators, led by Qatar and Pakistan, described progress as "significant" in efforts to revive an interim ceasefire deal signed in mid-June that collapsed amid the exchanges of fire (ap_india). Officials involved in the closed-door discussions said a compromise centers on a mechanism for managing vessel transit through the strait with fewer restrictions on shipping. Commercial traffic through the waterway fell to a three-week low during the height of tensions, though no new attacks had been confirmed in the preceding 72 hours as of Sunday (ap_india).

The fragile calm was tested by a flurry of drone activity across the region on Sunday and Monday. Saudi Arabia said it shot down drones fired by Iran-backed militias in Iraq targeting its petroleum facilities. Yemen's Houthi rebels, also backed by Iran, claimed attacks on Saudi oil infrastructure, while Jordan reported downing two drones of unspecified origin. Two separate drone strikes targeted a Kurdish Iranian dissident group in Iraq (ap_india). No casualties or damage were reported from these incidents.

The human toll of the recent escalation remained a point of contention in Washington. The Pentagon's official casualty system removed four U.S. soldiers killed during the renewed fighting from its "Iran war" tally last week, placing them in a new "Overseas Operations" category alongside 207 wounded personnel. The Defense Department attributed the shift to "anomalies" and "temporary data disruptions," though the move raised questions about the transparency of the conflict's human cost (ap_india).

With the 60-day window of the interim deal now in its second half, core issues—Iran's nuclear program chief among them—remain unaddressed. The market's current pricing signals that traders are weighing the durability of the pause and the resilience of supply routes against the low probability of a near-term diplomatic settlement that would return significant Iranian barrels to global markets (reuters_india).
