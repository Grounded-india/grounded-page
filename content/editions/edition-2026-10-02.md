### Context

### The debate

**The 'Cockroach' protesters:** The 'Cockroach' movement is not a fringe outburst but a necessary alarm raised by student coalitions and opposition forces in New Delhi and Mumbai against the systematic manipulation of voter rolls that has deleted legitimate citizens from the electoral register (ap_india, google_news_india, reuters_india). Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar bears direct responsibility for an institution that has lost public trust because it has failed to answer how names vanish without notice or audit trail. The demand for his resignation is the only proportional response when the guardian of the franchise becomes the instrument of its erosion.

**Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar:** The 'Cockroach' movement frames the demand for resignation as a proportional response to alleged voter roll manipulation, but offers no specific instance where the Commission acted outside the statutory framework governing electoral roll revisions. The Election Commission operates under the Representation of the People Act, 1950, and the Registration of Electors Rules, 1960, which mandate periodic revision, deletion of duplicate or ineligible entries, and a statutory objection-and-hearing process before any name is removed. Allegations of "deletion without notice or audit trail" contradict the legal requirement that every deletion be preceded by a notice in Form 7 and an opportunity to be heard, with appeals lying to the District Election Officer and the Chief Electoral Officer. The Commission has consistently maintained that the current revision cycle follows the same transparent, rule-bound procedure used in every election cycle since 1950, and has invited any aggrieved voter to file a claim under Form 6 or an objection under Form 7 at any designated location. Resignation of the Chief Election Commissioner would politicise a constitutional office designed to be insulated from precisely this kind of pressure, and would set a precedent that any protest — however unsubstantiated — can force the head of an independent body from office.

**The 'Cockroach' protesters (rebuttal):** The Commissioner invokes Form 7 notices and statutory hearings as proof of due process, but the protesters' core charge is that those very notices were never served or were served at wrong addresses, making the "opportunity to be heard" illusory for thousands of deleted voters (ap_india, google_news_india, reuters_india). Citing the rulebook does not rebut the allegation that the rulebook was ignored in practice. The Commission has not released aggregate data on how many Form 7 notices were actually delivered versus returned undelivered, nor has it opened its deletion logs for independent audit — transparency that would settle the dispute instantly if the process were truly clean. Student coalitions and opposition forces in New Delhi and Mumbai are demanding that accountability, not the office's politicisation.

**Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar (closing):** The protesters' rebuttal shifts from alleging "no process" to alleging "flawed execution" of the very statutory process they previously ignored, yet still offers no quantified evidence — no sample size, no district-level data, no audit report — to substantiate the claim that Form 7 notices were systematically undelivered. The Election Commission's deletion logs are maintained at the Electoral Registration Officer level and are open for inspection by any citizen under the Registration of Electors Rules; the remedy for a missed notice is a claim application under Form 6, which the Commission has publicly urged voters to file at any designated centre. Demanding the Chief Election Commissioner's resignation over unverified execution complaints in a single revision cycle, while the statutory correction mechanism remains open and unused by the complainants, conflates administrative grievance with constitutional crisis and threatens the insulation that allows the Commission to resist pressure from any quarter — including the very student coalitions and opposition forces now marching in New Delhi and Mumbai.

**Bottom line:** Protesters and the Election Commission agree that voter roll revisions are underway and that names have been removed, but they disagree on whether the legally mandated notice-and-hearing process was actually followed for those deletions. The protesters allege Form 7 notices were not delivered or sent to wrong addresses, making hearings illusory, while the Commission maintains the statutory framework was observed and deletion logs are open for inspection, though it has not released aggregate delivery data. The load-bearing question is whether the Commission will publish the notice-delivery and deletion records that would verify or falsify the claim of systematic procedural failure.

### Context

Nepal is moving to lower water levels in four high-altitude glacial lakes after a series of deadly floods highlighted the growing risks from melting ice. The decision follows recent catastrophic flooding events that killed dozens of people and destroyed infrastructure, which scientists link to accelerating glacial melt driven by climate change. Authorities plan to install early warning systems and siphon water from the most dangerous lakes to reduce pressure on natural dams. The effort reflects broader regional concerns as Himalayan glaciers shrink, increasing the frequency of glacial lake outburst floods across Nepal and neighboring countries.

### Report

Nepal will lower water levels in four high-altitude glacial lakes after a string of deadly outburst floods exposed the growing hazard of melting ice in the Himalayas, Reuters reported on Thursday. The decision follows catastrophic flooding events in recent months that killed dozens of people and destroyed roads, bridges and hydropower infrastructure, prompting authorities to accelerate mitigation work on the most unstable lakes.

Government officials said the plan involves installing siphon pipes to drain excess water and reduce pressure on the natural moraine dams that hold the lakes in place. Early warning sensors will also be deployed downstream to give communities more time to evacuate if a dam fails. The four lakes were selected based on satellite monitoring and field surveys that showed rapid expansion driven by accelerated glacial melt, a trend scientists link directly to rising temperatures across the Hindu Kush-Himalaya region.

Glacial lake outburst floods, or GLOFs, have become more frequent in Nepal and neighbouring countries as permanent ice fields shrink and leave behind growing bodies of meltwater held back by loose rock and sediment. When those dams breach — from erosion, seismic activity or sheer water pressure — the resulting surge can scour valleys kilometres downstream with little warning. The recent disasters have underscored the limits of existing monitoring networks and the urgency of structural interventions.

The drainage programme is expected to begin before the next monsoon season, though officials cautioned that high-altitude logistics, limited weather windows and the remoteness of the sites could slow implementation. International partners, including the World Bank and ICIMOD, have been consulted on technical design and funding. While the four lakes represent the most immediate threats, Nepal’s Department of Hydrology and Meteorology has identified more than 20 others classified as potentially dangerous, leaving open the question of how quickly the country can scale up its response across the wider Himalayan arc.

### Context

### Report

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said on Thursday that a trade deal with India is not imminent, telling Indian news agency ANI that the two sides have "truly identified the universe of items that are sticking points." The remarks came a day after President Donald Trump and Prime Minister Narendra Modi held what Greer described as a "very constructive" phone call, and as Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal conducted bilateral meetings in Washington ahead of the G20 trade ministers gathering in Milwaukee (indian_express, reuters_india, google_news_india).

Goyal struck a more optimistic tone in a social media post, saying discussions with Greer focused on the "early conclusion of a mutually beneficial interim agreement under the India-US Bilateral Trade Agreement." The contrast in framing underscores the gap between the two capitals. "Goyal is counting the issues already resolved, and Greer is counting the ones that remain," Ronak D. Desai, a visiting fellow at Stanford's Hoover Institution, told CNBC, adding that the final issues in any trade negotiation are typically the hardest (google_news_india).

For Washington, India's continued purchases of Russian oil remain a primary friction point. The U.S. administration argues that New Delhi's imports of Moscow's barrels fund the war in Ukraine, a stance that has already triggered tariff escalation. The U.S. imposed a 25% punitive tariff on India last August for buying Russian oil, raising total duties on Indian imports to 50%. In February, those duties were reduced to 18%, with Trump claiming India had "agreed to stop buying Russian Oil, and to buy much more from the United States and, potentially, Venezuela." New Delhi did not endorse those details, maintaining that energy security drives its purchasing decisions (google_news_india).

Since the start of the year, the U.S. and Venezuela combined have become more significant suppliers to India's energy basket, but global supply constraints linked to the Iran war have complicated diversification efforts. On India's side, the priority is protecting politically sensitive agricultural sectors and retaining preferential tariffs that give its exports an edge over competitors. Modi characterized his Wednesday conversation with Trump as "productive," noting the leaders reviewed trade, defense, energy, critical technologies, and efforts to advance global peace and security (google_news_india).

The trade talks now face the additional risk of Washington slapping tariffs of up to 100% on India for its Russian oil purchases, a move that could derail negotiations entirely. With the "universe of sticking points" mapped but unresolved, the timeline for even an interim agreement remains uncertain.
