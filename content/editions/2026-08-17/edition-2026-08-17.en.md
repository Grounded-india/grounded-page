### Context

Students in Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar have taken to the streets today to protest allegations of a paper leak in the High Court clerk examination, sparking demands for immediate investigation and accountability. The issue has gained urgency as the exam, a critical pathway for employment in the judiciary, faces credibility challenges amid claims of compromised integrity. Chief Justice of India (CJP) Dipke has weighed in, calling for swift action to address the concerns, reflecting the broader implications for public trust in institutional processes. The demonstrations underscore longstanding tensions around transparency and fairness in competitive exams across India.

### Report

Students gathered in the streets of Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar on Monday, waving banners and raising slogans demanding justice over allegations of a leaked question paper for the High Court clerk examination, according to reports from *Indian Express*. The protests, which began early in the day, drew hundreds of aspirants who claimed the leak had undermined the fairness of the exam, a critical gateway to employment in the judiciary. By midday, Chief Justice of India (CJP) Dipke had publicly intervened, urging authorities to address the situation promptly. “The integrity of such examinations must be upheld at all costs,” Dipke stated, without specifying further details (indian_express).

The alleged leak surfaced on social media platforms late Sunday, with users sharing screenshots of what they claimed were portions of the High Court clerk exam paper. Organizers of the protest asserted that the material matched the actual test administered earlier this month, though officials have not yet confirmed the authenticity of the leaked content. “We have evidence that parts of the paper were circulating before the exam,” said one student leader, who requested anonymity due to fear of retaliation. The Maharashtra State Board of Technical Education, which conducted the exam, has remained silent so far (indian_express).

The High Court clerk examination, held annually, attracts tens of thousands of applicants vying for a limited number of positions. For many, securing a role in the judiciary represents not only stable employment but also a chance to work within India’s legal framework. The current controversy has reignited debates about systemic vulnerabilities in competitive exams, a recurring issue that has plagued recruitment processes across states. In 2023, a similar leak in the Rajasthan Police constable exam led to widespread protests and a Supreme Court intervention. Experts argue that such incidents erode public trust in institutional fairness, particularly among youth already facing high unemployment rates (indian_express).

CJP Dipke’s intervention marks a rare instance of the judiciary directly addressing exam irregularities. While the CJP did not outline specific measures, legal analysts suggest the remark could pressure the concerned High Court or state authorities to initiate an inquiry. “This is a matter of credibility for the entire judicial system,” said senior advocate Rameshwar Prasad, speaking to *Indian Express*. “If aspirants lose faith in the process, it affects the very foundation of meritocracy.”  

As of Monday evening, no official statement has been released by the Maharashtra High Court or the state education department. Protesters have vowed to continue demonstrations until an independent investigation is announced. The timeline for any action remains unclear, leaving candidates in limbo. “We just want transparency,” one protester said. “If there was no leak, prove it. If there was, punish those responsible.” (indian_express)

### Report

Traders reported on Monday that the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has intensified its interventions in foreign exchange markets, with a "heavy footprint" signaling renewed efforts to stabilize the rupee amid growing pressures. The central bank’s actions, which include suspected dollar sales and currency swaps, have evoked comparisons to past periods of tight oversight, when the RBI actively managed the rupee’s trajectory to shield the economy from external volatility (Reuters, Google News India).

The Indian rupee, which has faced consistent downward pressure this year, is now expected to remain rangebound in the near term, according to market analysts. This outlook comes as bond traders await the release of the RBI’s latest policy minutes, which could provide clarity on the central bank’s broader strategy for monetary policy and currency management. Traders noted that the RBI’s recent interventions have been more overt than in recent months, with visible market activity suggesting a deliberate attempt to curb excessive depreciation (Reuters, Google News India).

Historically, the RBI has employed a range of tools to influence the rupee’s value, from direct market operations to regulatory measures on capital flows. The current wave of interventions has revived memories of the 2013 currency crisis, when the central bank aggressively defended the rupee during a period of global risk aversion. While such measures can provide short-term stability, they also raise questions about the long-term sustainability of a tightly managed exchange rate in an increasingly open economy. Analysts caution that prolonged intervention could drain foreign exchange reserves, though the RBI has so far avoided drastic steps like capital controls (Reuters).

The timing of the RBI’s actions coincides with broader economic uncertainties, including elevated oil prices and a weakening global trade environment. The rupee’s recent weakness has been exacerbated by a stronger U.S. dollar and domestic fiscal concerns, prompting the central bank to act. However, traders remain divided on whether the current strategy will suffice. Some argue that without addressing underlying economic fundamentals, such as trade deficits and inflation, the rupee will remain vulnerable to market sentiment (Google News India).

As the week unfolds, market participants will closely watch the RBI’s policy minutes for signals on future interventions and monetary tightening. The central bank has not yet commented publicly on its recent forex operations, leaving traders to interpret the scale and intent of its actions. What remains clear is that the rupee’s trajectory will hinge on both domestic policy decisions and global macroeconomic trends, with the RBI walking a delicate line between stability and market autonomy (Reuters).
