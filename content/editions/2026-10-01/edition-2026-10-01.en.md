### Context

The Election Commission of India has extended the dates for the Summary Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls in Andhra Pradesh and Meghalaya. This revision is a routine but critical exercise to update voter lists by adding new eligible voters, correcting entries, and removing names of deceased or shifted electors before elections. The extension provides additional time for citizens in these two states to file claims and objections, ensuring the rolls are as accurate as possible ahead of upcoming electoral cycles.

### Report

A full bench of the Election Commission of India convened on Wednesday for the second time in four days and decided to push back the publication of final electoral rolls in Andhra Pradesh and Meghalaya, the two states currently undergoing a special intensive revision (SIR) of voter lists. The final voters’ list for Andhra Pradesh will now be published on October 16 instead of October 3, while Meghalaya’s roll will be finalised on October 26 rather than October 7, according to details released after the meeting (the_hindu).

Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar and Election Commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi attended the session, which also reviewed the implementation of corrective measures announced on September 26. Those measures followed reported dissent notes from Commissioners Sandhu and Joshi regarding the SIR process. Among the steps then announced, the Commission halted physical hearings for voters flagged under unmapped or logical discrepancy categories and restricted a declaration attached to Form 6 — used for new voter registration — so that it applies only in states where the SIR is actively underway. That declaration was de-linked from non-SIR states on Tuesday evening (the_hindu).

The claims and objections period in Andhra Pradesh has been extended until October 10, giving citizens additional time to challenge entries. The Commission also confirmed that a committee headed by a Senior Deputy Election Commissioner, including an independent expert from an IIT or IIIT, will review the ECINet portal to address concerns about its centralisation in handling voter rolls (the_hindu).

Separately, the Chief Electoral Officer of Delhi reported that block-level officers have begun house-to-house visits to collect documents in cases where notices were issued. The PIB confirmed the extensions for both states without specifying the new dates (pib). It remains unclear whether further revisions to the timeline will be needed before the final rolls are published.

### Context

India is considering reducing import duties on pulses to help lower domestic prices, according to sources cited by Reuters. The potential move comes as the government seeks to cool food inflation by making imported pulses cheaper and increasing domestic supply.

### Report

India is considering a reduction in import duties on pulses as part of efforts to temper domestic food inflation, according to government sources cited by Reuters on Wednesday. The potential policy shift aims to make overseas shipments cheaper and boost local supply at a time when protein prices remain a persistent component of the consumer price basket.

The deliberations come against a backdrop of sticky food inflation that has complicated the Reserve Bank of India’s monetary easing cycle. While headline inflation cooled slightly in August, driven partly by a favourable base effect and moderating vegetable prices, the price of pulses—a primary protein source for a significant portion of the population—has remained elevated due to lower domestic output in the previous crop year and tight global supplies (reuters_india).

Current import duties vary by variety; yellow peas attract a 50% tariff, while lentils and chickpeas face duties of 33% and 66% respectively. A cut would effectively lower the landed cost of imports from major suppliers such as Canada, Australia, and Myanmar, potentially transmitting lower prices to wholesale mandis and retail markets ahead of the key festival season (reuters_india).

The move also intersects with broader external sector concerns. A separate government report highlighted this week that geopolitical tensions and elevated crude oil prices risk fuelling imported inflation, limiting the space for aggressive fiscal or monetary stimulus (reuters_india). Cheaper pulse imports could offset some of that pressure on the food sub-index without requiring a drawdown on buffer stocks, which the government has been rebuilding through procurement at minimum support prices.

Officials cautioned that no final decision has been taken and that the timing and magnitude of any duty reduction are still under discussion. The Department of Consumer Affairs and the Ministry of Finance are evaluating the revenue implication against the expected disinflationary impact. A formal notification could be issued as early as the coming weeks if consensus is reached.

The court's insistence on a technical explanation suggests a judicial focus on the robustness of the software and verification protocols used for roll maintenance.

The Election Commission has previously maintained that the cleaning of electoral rolls is a continuous, technology-driven exercise aimed at removing duplicate and deceased entries. However, the court's intervention signals a requirement for transparency regarding the specific failure points in that automation. The matter is expected to be heard again once the Commission submits its affidavit explaining the nature of the error and the corrective steps taken to restore the names.

## 21. Why Gandhi Chose to Spend Independence Day in Riot-Hit Calcutta

*An exploration of the historical reasons behind Mahatma Gandhi's decision to spend Independence Day in Calcutta amid ongoing riots.*

> REPORT  ·  1 sources  ·  1 claim(s) kept  ·  0 verified

### Context

Mahatma Gandhi chose to spend India's first Independence Day on August 15, 1947, in Calcutta rather than join the celebrations in New Delhi. He was there to quell violent Hindu-Muslim riots that had engulfed the city, undertaking a fast to restore communal peace while the rest of the country marked the transfer of power.

### Report

On August 15, 1947, as New Delhi prepared for the formal transfer of power and the unfurling of the national flag, Mahatma Gandhi was in Calcutta. He had refused repeated invitations to attend the ceremonies in the capital, choosing instead to remain in a city convulsed by Hindu-Muslim violence that had flared violently in the days leading up to Independence (indian_express).

The decision was not symbolic withdrawal but active intervention. Calcutta had suffered severe communal riots in August 1946 and again in the summer of 1947, leaving hundreds dead and neighbourhoods segregated by fear. Gandhi arrived in the city on August 9, taking up residence at Hydari Manzil in the Beliaghata area, a Muslim-majority locality bordering Hindu neighbourhoods. From there, he held daily prayer meetings, met delegations from both communities, and walked through affected streets to assess the damage and morale (indian_express).

On the morning of August 15, while Jawaharlal Nehru delivered his "Tryst with Destiny" speech in the Constituent Assembly, Gandhi spent the day spinning, praying, and meeting visitors. He did not hoist the flag. In a statement issued that day, he wrote that he could not rejoice while "the two communities are at each other's throats" and while the shadow of partition violence lengthened across Bengal and Punjab. He announced a fast unto death beginning that evening, vowing not to eat until "sanity returns to Calcutta" (indian_express).

The fast had immediate effect. Hindu and Muslim leaders, including Suhrawardy, the Muslim League Premier of Bengal, visited Gandhi at Hydari Manzil. Crowds gathered outside the house, many weeping, surrendering weapons, and pledging peace. By August 18, communal leaders signed a joint declaration guaranteeing the safety of minorities and the cessation of violence. Gandhi broke his fast on the third day after accepting the pledge, though he warned that the peace must be sustained by the people themselves, not merely by his presence (indian_express).

Gandhi's absence from Delhi was noted by the new government. Lord Mountbatten, the last Viceroy, later remarked that Gandhi's "one-man boundary force" in Calcutta had achieved more than the military deployments in Punjab. Historians have since cited the Calcutta fast as a rare instance where moral authority halted large-scale violence, even as the subcontinent burned elsewhere. The question of whether such moral leverage could be replicated in the weeks that followed, as partition massacres accelerated in the west, remains a subject of debate (indian_express).
